### How to Detect?

This KQL (Kusto Query Language) query is designed to detect a specific type of phishing attack known as "Sneaky 2FA" (Two-Factor Authentication) that uses phishing-as-a-service techniques. Here's a simplified breakdown of what the query does:

* Load Suspicious Domains: It retrieves a list of suspicious domains related to the Sneaky 2FA attack from an external data source (a GitHub repository).
* Extract Unique Domains: It extracts distinct domains from the loaded data to use as indicators of compromise (IOCs).

Identify Inbound Email Recipients:

* It identifies unique email addresses that have received inbound emails by joining two tables: EmailUrlInfo and EmailEvents.
* It also creates a base64-encoded version of these email addresses for further analysis.

Filter and Analyze Emails:

* It examines inbound emails that have been delivered.
* It checks if the URLs in these emails match any of the suspicious domains (IOCs).
* Additionally, it verifies if these URLs contain either the plain or encoded version of the recipient's email address.

The goal of this query is to detect potentially malicious emails that are part of the Sneaky 2FA phishing campaign by looking for specific patterns in email URLs and recipient information.

### KQL

```kql
    let Sneaky2FATable=externaldata(RawData:string)
    [h'https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SlimKQL/Hunting-Queries-Detection-Rules/refs/heads/main/IOC/Sneaky2FA.txt']
    | parse RawData with Sneaky2FADomains:string;
    let IOCs =
    Sneaky2FATable
    | distinct Sneaky2FADomains;
    let InboundEmailReceipient =
    EmailUrlInfo
    | join EmailEvents on NetworkMessageId
    | where Timestamp > ago(1d)
    | where EmailDirection == "Inbound"
    | distinct RecipientEmailAddress;
    let InboundEncodedEmailReceipient =
    EmailUrlInfo
    | join EmailEvents on NetworkMessageId
    | where Timestamp > ago(1d)
    | where EmailDirection == "Inbound"
    | extend EncodeEmail = base64_encode_tostring(RecipientEmailAddress)
    | distinct EncodeEmail;
    EmailUrlInfo
    | join EmailEvents on NetworkMessageId
    | where Timestamp > ago(1d)
    | where EmailDirection == "Inbound"
    | where DeliveryAction == "Delivered"
    | where Url has_any(IOCs) and (Url has_any(InboundEmailReceipient) or Url has_any(InboundEncodedEmailReceipient))
```

## How to Investigate?

1. Identify the Scope of the Incident
    * Determine which inbound emails contained URLs matching the Sneaky2FA domain list.
    * Identify how many unique recipients received these suspicious URLs.
    * Assess whether the impacted users belong to high-risk roles (admins, finance, executives).

2. Validate the Malicious Domains
    * Confirm whether the domains in the Sneaky2FA IOC list are known phishing or MFA-bypass infrastructure.
    * Check threat intelligence sources for domain classification, risk scores, and known campaigns.
    * Determine whether these domains were seen previously in your environment.

3. Review Email Characteristics
    * Analyze subject lines, senders, and email content associated with the flagged messages.
    * Check whether the sender addresses are spoofed or newly observed.
    * Determine if the messages show typical phishing indicators (urgency, impersonation, login requests).

4. Assess User Interaction
    * Identify whether any recipients clicked the URLs in the phishing emails.
    * Determine whether users attempted to authenticate on any suspicious pages.
    * Look for risky behavior such as multiple clicks or repeated access attempts.

5. Evaluate Potential Compromise
    * Review authentication logs for impacted users around the time the emails were delivered.
    * Look for unusual sign-in attempts, geographic anomalies, or MFA failures.
    * Check for new devices, browsers, or sessions associated with the accounts.

6. Examine Delivery and Filtering Gaps
    * Determine why these phishing messages were delivered and not blocked or quarantined.
    * Identify which filters, policies, or SafeLinks/SafeAttachments defenses may have been bypassed.
    * Assess whether this indicates a gap in email security configuration.

7. Inspect Related Activity on Devices
    * Check whether any devices used by targeted users show additional indicators of phishing exploitation.
    * Look for credential theft tools, browser injections, or suspicious network traffic.
