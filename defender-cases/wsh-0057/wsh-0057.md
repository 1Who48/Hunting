## WSH-0057 Fake PDF Editors as Initial Access

## Overview

Since November 19, 2025, a significant increase in security alerts related to a file named ConvertMate. Although the program presented itself as a legitimate PDF-editing tool, its behavior during execution quickly revealed suspicious activity. Once launched, ConvertMate initiated outbound network connections, performed host lookups, and dropped multiple artifacts onto the system—including updating_files.zip, native.zip, UpdateRetriever.exe, and a PowerShell script named conmate_update.ps1.

The PowerShell script played a central role in persistence, automatically creating a scheduled task that forced UpdateRetriever.exe to execute every 24 hours. Each execution repeated the same pattern of external communication, contacting a series of hard-coded domains such as confetly[.]com and conmateapp[.]com, indicating command-and-control or payload retrieval behavior.

By November 24 and 25, Truesec identified additional related binaries—such as pdfclick.exe and PDFClickUpdater.exe—each with new SHA-256 hashes. This demonstrated that the threat actors were rapidly iterating on their tooling and possibly deploying multiple variants in parallel. Investigators also discovered that the executables were all signed using the same digital certificate issued to AMARYLLIS SIGNAL LTD, the same certificate used in a previous malicious PDF-editor campaign earlier in 2025, further linking the two operations.
In conclusion, ConvertMate is not a genuine PDF editor at all, but a carefully crafted initial access vector designed to infiltrate environments, establish persistent footholds, and enable further malicious activity.

## How to Investigate?

* If the KQL hits, isolate the affected device immediately (network + host).
* Inspect running scheduled tasks (especially tasks referencing UpdateRetriever.exe, ConvertMate.exe, or similar names).
* Review outbound network connections from the time of execution: domains like the ones listed above may indicate C2 activity.
* Search for other dropped artifacts (updating_files.zip, native.zip, conmate_update.ps1) in filesystem and memory.
* Cross-check installed certificates on executables — suspicious or previously known malicious signers (e.g. “AMARYLLIS SIGNAL LTD”) may hint at trojanized software.
* Perform full EDR / EDR log review: registry changes, persistence mechanism, process injection, etc.
