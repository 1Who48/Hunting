## WSH-0407 Sentinel UEBA to safeguard against OpenSSH vulnerability exploits

## Document Classification

This document is only for internal use of Wortell Managed Detection and Response.

## Overview

Enhance protection against OpenSSH vulnerability exploits, specifically CVE-2024-6387 (hashtag#RegreSSHion) and CVE-2024-6409 (hashtag#Privsep).

## Applicability & Technical Requirements

This use case is applicable to customers that subscribe to the Wortell MDR **"Identity Protect Cloud"**

### How to detect?

The **"AzureActivity"** logs of the customer need to be exported to the Wortell MDR Sentinel Log Analytics Workspace with the Microsoft Sentinel **"Azure Activity"** connector of Sentinel.

### KQL

```kql
    BehaviorAnalytics
    | where TimeGenerated > ago(1d)
    | extend ThreatIntelIndicatorDescription = DevicesInsights.ThreatIntelIndicatorDescription
    | where isnotempty( ThreatIntelIndicatorDescription ) 
    | where ActivityType !has "FailedLogOn"
    | where ThreatIntelIndicatorDescription contains "destination_port_numbers: 22" and ThreatIntelIndicatorDescription contains "A BruteForce indicator"
    | distinct SourceIPAddress, SourceIPLocation
    | sort by SourceIPLocation asc
```

## How to investigate?

1. Validate the threat intelligence signal context
   - Confirm that the indicator explicitly references SSH (port 22) brute-force activity and is not a generic enrichment
   - Review the time window in which the indicator was observed and how recently it was updated
   - Determine whether the indicator is sourced from first-party telemetry, third-party feeds, or aggregated intelligence

2. Assess the source IP and geographic consistency
   - Review the SourceIPAddress and associated geolocation to determine whether it aligns with known infrastructure, VPNs, or cloud providers
   - Check for anomalies such as unexpected countries, rapid location changes, or mismatches with known business operations
   - Determine whether multiple IPs map to the same region or ASN, suggesting coordinated activity

3. Correlate with authentication and access behavior
   - Review successful and non-failed authentication activity involving port 22 from the same source IPs
   - Determine whether any accounts or devices show unusual access patterns following the indicator
   - Check whether activity types suggest interactive access, automated tooling, or service-based connections

4. Identify impacted assets and exposure
   - Determine which internal devices or workloads were targeted by the source IPs
   - Assess whether the destination systems normally expose SSH and whether that exposure is expected
   - Review asset roles (servers, network devices, appliances) to understand potential impact relevance

5. Look for escalation or follow-on activity
   - Check for subsequent behavior associated with lateral movement, privilege changes, or new process execution tied to the same sources
   - Review whether activity persists over time or escalates in frequency or scope
   - Determine whether the behavior transitions from reconnaissance to access-related actions

6. Establish scope and pattern over time
   - Assess whether the same source IPs appear across multiple days or environments
   - Identify whether activity clusters around specific timeframes or follows a predictable cadence
   - Document whether the behavior appears opportunistic, targeted, or part of a broader campaign
